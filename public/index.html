Offline password protection

Encryption that never touches a computer

The strongest key you own is the one that was never typed, never synced and never backed up. Answer eight questions and get a ranked, honest plan for protecting your most important passwords with paper, dice and a pen.

    What this tool will not pretend

    • Classical ciphers are not strong encryption. Vigenere, Playfair and transposition have been breakable by skilled people since the nineteenth century. They are listed because stopping a burglar is a real and useful goal - not because they would survive an expert.
    • A one-time pad is only unbreakable if you follow the rules. Truly random, never reused, as long as the secret, and stored apart from the ciphertext. Break any one of those and it degrades to something much weaker.
    • Paper fails in ordinary ways. Fire, damp, faded ink, house moves and helpful relatives destroy more offline secrets than cryptanalysis ever has. That is why storing and verifying are stages here, not afterthoughts.
    • Offline is not automatically safer. A password manager with a strong master password beats a badly kept sheet of paper. This is for the small number of secrets that genuinely should never be on a computer at all: the master password itself, seed phrases, recovery codes, and what your family will need.
    • Nothing here is legal or financial advice, and no method survives someone standing over you demanding a password.
